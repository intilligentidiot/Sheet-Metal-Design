# 5 Questions to Ask Engineering Design Outsourcing Partner Before Signing an NDA

I've sat on both sides of this table. As the vendor being vetted, and as the one doing the vetting when we've brought in outside help for overflow work.

Most companies get the NDA signed fast because it feels like the responsible first step. Then three months in, they find out the question that actually mattered wasn't on it.

Here are five worth asking before you sign anything.

1\. Do they actually know your industry, or just engineering in general?

Mechanical design for a medical device and mechanical design for a consumer appliance are not the same job, even if the CAD tools look identical. Standards differ. ASME, ISO, CE marking, whatever applies to your product, someone on the team needs to have lived with those requirements before.

Don't accept "we work across industries" as an answer. Ask for a specific project, a specific standard they designed to, and what went wrong on it. Real experience comes with a story about a mistake. A rehearsed pitch usually doesn't.

2\. Do you get the native CAD files, or just the outputs?

This is the one that gets skipped most often, and it costs the most later.

Some firms hand over a finished drawing or a STEP file and keep the native parametric model, the full design history, the configurations, sitting on their own servers. If you ever need to modify that design without going back through them, you're stuck.

Get it in writing before the NDA: full native files, editable, delivered at project milestones, not just dropped at the very end.

3\. Is IP ownership actually assigned, or just implied?

An NDA protects confidentiality. It doesn't automatically transfer ownership of what gets created. Those are two separate legal functions doing two separate jobs, and a lot of people assume the NDA covers both.

Ask directly whether the contract includes a work-for-hire or IP assignment clause, and whether it covers derivative designs, not just the final file. I've seen companies find out the hard way that ownership was never actually spelled out.

4\. What happens if the software doesn't match?

If your team runs SolidWorks and they build everything in Creo, that mismatch shows up as lost associativity, broken references, and hours spent cleaning up a file that should've just opened correctly.

Ask what they standardize on, and what their process looks like when it doesn't match yours. A firm with a real answer has hit this problem before and built a workflow around it.

5\. What does a mid-project exit actually look like?

Nobody wants to ask this before the relationship has even started. Ask it anyway.

Find out whether you'd get a clean handoff of work-in-progress files, whether there's a notice period, and whether their documentation is good enough that a different engineer could pick the project up without starting from zero. A firm that answers this clearly has been through it before. One that gets defensive is telling you something too.

Why this matters more than the NDA itself

The NDA is necessary. It's also just a piece of paper. The real protection is knowing how a partner actually operates, and whether their answers to these five hold up when you push for specifics instead of reassurance.

If they answer all five straight, that's a good sign no matter who they are. If they dodge even one, you've learned something worth knowing before you've committed anything.
